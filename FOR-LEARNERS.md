# For Learners

You are learning to code. That is already more than most people do. Here is the honest version of
how VCL fits into your journey.

## The short version

VCL is a mindset. Your `LICENSE` is the law.

- VCL does not grant you rights to anyone else's code.
- VCL does not protect your code from being copied.
- VCL does not replace MIT, Apache-2.0, or any other real license.
- VCL is a manifesto you can put next to your real license.

## A safe setup for your first public repo

1. Pick a real license. If you don't know which one, use MIT.
2. Put the full MIT text in `LICENSE` with your name and the current year.
3. Copy [`templates/VIBE.md`](./templates/VIBE.md) to `VIBE.md`.
4. Add a line to your README:
   `Code: [MIT](./LICENSE). Vibes: [Vibe Coding License 1.0](./VIBE.md) — a manifesto, not a license.`
5. Do not rename `VIBE.md` to `LICENSE`.
6. Do not use code from the internet without checking its license.
7. AI-generated code is not automatically free of other people's rights. Check dependency licenses
   and the terms of the tool you use.
8. Never commit secrets, tokens, or `.env` files.
9. Tests are not optional. "It works on my machine" is a meme, not a QA strategy.

## What VCL actually gives you

A vibe. A slogan. A sticker. A way to signal that you ship with confidence and a sense of humor.

## What it does not give you

Legal permission. A warranty. A patent grant. A liability shield. A replacement for a real license.

## If you are under 18

Ask a parent, teacher, or maintainer before publishing code with your real name, school, or
contact details. You can still use the manifesto; just keep your personal data out of the repo.

## The one rule

Ship something. Then learn why it broke. Then ship again.
